Born for the depths, worn for a lifetime. Oystersteel, a two‑colour Cerachrom bezel, and three hundred metres of quiet confidence.

View on Rolex.com

Introduced in 1953 as the first wristwatch waterproof to 100 metres, the Submariner set the standard every dive watch since has been measured against. This green and black edition carries the nickname collectors gave it long before Rolex ever printed it on a box — earned by a bezel that shifts colour with the light.

A name earned, not given

Rolex called it simply the green Submariner. It was the buyers who named it: Kermit at launch in 2003 for its all‑green bezel, then Starbucks when the black half arrived in 2020, after the two colours together happened to match a coffee cup people saw every morning. Neither name appears anywhere on the watch. Both stuck anyway.

Built for someone who needed it

Long before it sat in a display case, this was a tool handed to divers who could not afford a watch that guessed. The rotating bezel tracks elapsed time underwater with a single glance, the Mercedes hands stay legible at a depth where colour disappears, and the case keeps working long after the wearer has stopped thinking about it. That brief hasn't changed since 1953 — only the ceramics and the calibre inside have.

What's on the wrist

Case

Bezel

Dial

Bracelet
Jubilee, five‑piece links in Oystersteel with a folding Oysterlock safety clasp
Movement
Calibre 3235, self‑winding, roughly 70 hours of power reserve
Certification
Superlative Chronometer, tested as a complete watch to −2/+2 seconds a day

Why the bezel never fades

Most watches lose their colour to sunlight within a few years. The Submariner's Cerachrom insert is milled from a ceramic powder, coloured, then fired at extreme heat until it hardens into something closer to sapphire than paint — scratch‑resistant, and immune to UV. The numerals are formed the same way: a thin layer of platinum, physical vapour deposited into the ceramic itself, rather than printed on top of it. It is slower and far more expensive to produce than a painted bezel. It is also the reason a thirty‑year‑old Submariner can still look new.
